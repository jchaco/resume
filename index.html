Featured Research

Two first-author studies connect blood metabolite profiles with clinical measurements and longitudinal outcomes. They are research evaluations of biomarkers and prediction models.

01

Preprint · under review

  • 54,933 participants
  • 250 NMR biomarkers

Cardiovascular–kidney–metabolic risk stratification

An NMR blood profile was evaluated in 54,933 Kaiser Permanente Research Bank participants to test whether 250 biomarkers add predictive value beyond standard clinical risk models. Endpoints cover nine cardiovascular–kidney–metabolic diseases and downstream complications, ascertained from longitudinal electronic health records.

For selected outcomes, discrimination improved by approximately ΔC-index +0.05 to +0.08. The study assessed calibration and decision-curve clinical net benefit, and compared the NMR profile with polygenic risk scores.

Methods include penalized survival models, EHR phenotyping, and subgroup analyses. Models were transported from UK Biobank (248,006 participants) to Kaiser Permanente while addressing shifts in metabolite distributions. This is a 2026 medRxiv preprint; the manuscript is under review.

02

Peer-reviewed · 2026

  • 979 participants
  • 636 LC-MS metabolites
  • Cardiac MRI and echocardiography
  • Circulation Research

Right ventricular metabolomics in pulmonary hypertension

In the NIH-funded multicenter PVDOMICS cohort, untargeted LC-MS measured 636 metabolites in 979 participants. Right ventricular function was characterized with cardiac MRI and echocardiography.

170 metabolites were associated with all three right ventricular function measures studied. Implicated pathways include vitamin A, glutathione and oxidative stress, fatty acids, and steroid hormones. The analysis examined heterogeneity by pulmonary hypertension subgroup, hemodynamics, and sex.

LASSO metabolite scores for the right ventricular measurements showed prognosis for death or transplant comparable to the underlying imaging measurements. The scores are a research evaluation alongside those measurements.

Also investigating

Treatment response

Ongoing work uses clinical records and molecular profiles to predict responders and non-responders to GLP-1 receptor agonists and statins, in UK Biobank and Kaiser Permanente.

Genetic bias in HbA1c

With the Cornell Center for Health Equity, a Kaiser Permanente analysis quantifies how known inherited variants can shift HbA1c, including G6PD-related underestimation enriched in certain genetic ancestries. Alternative NMR-based risk scores were evaluated for genetic bias, finding no significant genetic bias and improved diabetes risk prediction over HbA1c-based models in at-risk subgroups (manuscript in preparation).
